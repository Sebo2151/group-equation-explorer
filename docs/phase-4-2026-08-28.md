# Phase 4 — Curriculum and progression

Implemented 2026-08-28, on top of the Phase 3 equation workbench and the menu
shell that preceded it.

The plan asked for teaching modes, curated dependency-checked challenges,
graduated hints, unlocks, a proof viewer, personal bests, and device-local
persistence with export and reset, and set three acceptance conditions: no
circular proofs or assumption leakage, scores comparable only within their
recorded ruleset, and credit for valid alternative proofs.

## What a learner sees

The menu now runs in order and holds the learner to it. Challenge 01 is open;
everything after it says which challenge it is waiting for, and pressing it goes
there rather than doing nothing. A finished challenge shows a tick, its length,
and how that length compares to the proof the app ships with, and it can be
reopened read-only. Below the list is what the device remembers: the laws earned
so far, where each was proved, and controls to copy or clear the lot.

Inside a proof there is a Hint control, and on finishing, a note saying what the
proof cost and what it earned.

## Decisions

### Lemmas are proved before they are used

Challenges 03 and 04 previously handed over `(a^-1)^-1 = a` and
`(ab)^-1 = b^-1a^-1` as laws and then asked the learner to apply them. Each is
now a pair: a challenge whose goal *is* the lemma, proved from the axioms, and a
separate challenge that uses it. The same is true of `e^-1 = e`, which was not a
challenge at all before, and of cancellation, which Phase 3 had deliberately
left out of the catalogue.

The curriculum is now nineteen challenges rather than eleven:

```text
01  Cancel what undoes itself
02  Put something in
03  Prove: the identity inverts to itself      earns  Invert the identity
04  Prove: an inverse of an inverse            earns  Undo / write a double inverse
05  Undo an undoing
06  Prove: socks and shoes                     earns  Distribute / collect an inverse
07  Socks and shoes
08  Reach inside
09  Powers are shorthand
10  Power workout
11  Solve for x
12  The other side
13  Read it the other way
14  Inverses of equals
15  Prove: cancel a common factor              earns  Cancel on the left / right
16  Cancel from both ends
17  Mixed inverses
18  Solve from both ends
19  Solve where it stands
```

Cancellation becoming a law is new. Phase 3 recorded that it should ultimately
be unlockable, and an unlock system whose final challenge earns nothing would be
hollow, so `cancel-left` and `cancel-right` were added to the equation
catalogue — available to nobody until challenge 15 has been worked.

### Every law is either primitive or earned, and a test says which

`PRIMITIVE_RULES` names the laws a learner may use before earning anything: the
axioms (cancelling and inserting an inverse pair, removing and inserting the
identity), the whole-equation moves, and the power laws. The power laws are
there because they are the *definition* of `x^n` rather than theorems about it;
making somebody derive a definition would teach the wrong thing about where
notation comes from. `invert-both-sides` is the one judgement call, and it is
recorded as one: it rests on inverses being unique, which is a theorem, but a
theorem about equations, and this app proves things about equations by
transforming them — there is no sequence of moves here that could establish it.

Everything else must be granted by an earlier challenge. A test walks the list
and fails if any challenge offers a derived law before the challenge that proves
it, if a challenge is given the law it exists to prove, or if a law is earned
twice or never. That is the "no circular proofs" criterion, checked rather than
asserted.

Two further tests keep the pairs honest: a lemma challenge must become shorter
if it were given its own lemma, and an application challenge must become
unsolvable without it.

### The kernel had to change for socks-and-shoes to be provable

Deriving `(ab)^-1 = b^-1a^-1` from the axioms means writing `(ab)^-1(ab)` and
cancelling it. Products are flat, so that line is stored as the three factors
`(ab)^-1`, `a`, `b`, and `cancel-inverse` only ever matched two adjacent
factors — so the lemma could not be proved at all.

`cancel-inverse` now also matches a factor against the adjacent run it inverts,
at either end. This removes a limitation of the storage format rather than
granting a new law: the plan already says associative rebracketing is free and
must never cost a step, and `a b (ab)^-1` *is* an instance of `x x^-1` with the
grouping hidden. The generalisation is deliberately narrow — one end of the run
must invert exactly the rest, in order. `b^-1 a^-1 a b` equals the identity and
still does not cancel in one move, because seeing that would mean reasoning
about products rather than matching a term against its own inverse.

One consequence: the old challenge 04, `(ab)^-1 a b → e`, became a one-step
exercise. The applying challenge is now `(ab)^-1 a → b^-1`, where the shortcut
does not apply.

### Goals can be a shape, in exactly one way

A goal is either an exact subject — unchanged, and still orientation sensitive,
so reaching `v = u` when the goal is `u = v` leaves symmetry to be spent — or
"this variable, alone on this side". Challenges 11 to 13 use the shape, so a
learner who clears the `a` by another route still finishes. That is the
acceptance criterion about alternative proofs, made true rather than hoped for.

The shape requires the variable to be *gone from the other side*, not merely
alone on this one: `x = x b` is not solved for `x`.

Only this one shape. A general pattern language for goals is a larger surface to
test and to explain than the curriculum needs.

### Hints walk an authored route, and admit when they cannot

Each challenge ships a reference proof, written as "this law, at the nth place
it offers", and machine-checked against exactly that challenge's own tools. The
hints walk it in three grades: the family to look in, then the law and where it
applies, then the move itself as a control. A test takes the strongest hint over
and over on every challenge and requires it to finish, so "nobody is stuck" is a
fact rather than an intention.

There is no proof search. Searching from wherever a learner happens to be is
Phase 6 work and would be unreliable here anyway — the insertion laws have
infinitely many instantiations, so a search would need a pool of guessed terms
and could fail to find a proof that plainly exists. A hint that quietly means "I
gave up" is worse than no hint.

That leaves the learner who has gone somewhere else, whose line may be perfectly
correct. The app says so — "you have gone a different way from the route I know;
nothing is wrong with your line" — and offers the two useful things: show the
route it knows, or step back onto it.

### Progress is stored as proofs, not as flags

For each challenge the device keeps the shortest proof of it that it has seen.
Everything the interface shows is derived from that: what is unlocked, which
laws are earned, what a personal best is, what can be read back.

This is the answer to "persisted data must be validated, not trusted as unlock
evidence". A stored `{"cancellation": true}` could be typed into a console by
anyone; a stored proof could not. Reading progress replays every record through
the same rule contracts the interface uses, against the challenge's own frozen
start, goal and ruleset, and drops anything that does not check out — including
a genuine proof relabelled with a shorter step count, because the count stored
beside a record must be the one the replay produces. Editing the file to unlock
something is exactly as much work as proving the theorem.

One bad entry costs that entry and not the file, and the menu says how many were
dropped rather than quietly showing less progress than the learner remembers.

A best only displaces a best when both were made with the same tools. Four steps
with cancellation available is not the same achievement as four steps without
it, so a proof under a different ruleset replaces the record outright rather
than competing with it, and the ruleset travels with the score.

The benchmark a score is read against is the authored proof, and it is described
as exactly that — "shorter than the proof this app ships with", never "the
shortest there is". Nothing here does an exhaustive search, so nothing here
claims a global minimum.

### A locked challenge still opens by link

The menu enforces the order. A link does not: somebody meant to send it, and
every challenge staying linkable is what the menu shell was built to guarantee.
Arriving that way says so — "you have jumped ahead: this one normally opens once
X is done" — rather than pretending it was the intended route.

### Teaching modes were not built as modes

The plan proposed Guided, Practice and Free, and noted the Practice interaction
was unsettled. Guidance turned out to be a property of a challenge rather than a
mode of the app: hints are available on every challenge, at whatever grade the
learner asks for, and free exploration already exists as its own destination.
Three named modes would have been a menu of three doors onto two behaviours.

### Chapters make the learning arc visible

The course map is grouped into Foundations, Inverse Theorems, Powers,
Equations, and Fluency. Each chapter states its outcome, while each challenge
adds an objective, a pre-proof thinking prompt, and a post-proof takeaway. Four
new practice and transfer challenges close gaps in the earlier sequence:
powers receive a second exercise, inverse laws are combined in a fresh context,
and equation solving advances to factors on both ends and a reversed equation.
The dashboard now foregrounds the next useful challenge and overall progress,
with the detailed course map still available chapter by chapter.

## What is not done

- **Proof records are version 3 and there is no migration.** Nothing has shipped
  to students, so an old record is simply refused.
- **Progress does not travel between devices.** It can be copied out as JSON,
  but there is no import for it — a learner moving machines starts again. The
  proof-record import already covers sharing a single proof.
- **`standing()` distinguishes beaten, matched and proved, but the interface
  only uses the first two.** A proof longer than the benchmark reads as its
  length beside the benchmark, which says the same thing more usefully.
- **No challenge yet uses the goal shape outside the solve-for-x group**, and no
  challenge has more than one reference proof, so "the route I know" is always
  singular.

## Evidence

- 251 unit tests (`npm test`), including new suites for goals, progress and
  hints, and additions to the rule and soundness suites for the widened
  cancellation and the two new cancellation laws. Every rule is still checked by
  evaluation in S3 and D4 as well as structurally.
- 16 browser tests for this phase (`tests/browser/phase4.spec.ts`), across both
  the desktop and 390px projects, covering unlocking, earned laws, the three
  hint grades, divergence, reading a proof back, focus on arrival, goal shapes,
  and two storage forgeries a curious student would actually try.
- The tamper cases were also confirmed by hand in the running app: a forged
  completion and a relabelled proof both left the course locked, with the app
  saying which records it had dropped.
